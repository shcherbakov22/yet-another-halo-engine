## Wins that got us here

One line each: what, the measured effect, when the set was current. Prefill numbers are pp2048 `layers_ms` or cycles; decode numbers ms per token.

Prefill:

- Activations straight from global memory instead of an LDS tile (`Tile.afrag`; on for the IQ3_S / IQ3_XXS / IQ4_XS FFN GEMMs and the Q3_K ffn_gate since 2026-10-03, knobs per GEMM in `emit_prefill_pp.AF`; since the same day also the DeltaNet qkv / gate, the K = 6144 o-proj / ssm_out and Q4_K / Q3_K where they win (attention output, postnorm and attn_norm write the fragment-major input), and the attention q (kqg: the tall epilogue with the q / gate split; the footprint gate had refused the fragment-store fallback, which wrote the rows unsplit) and k / v (Q5_K / Q6_K with `ksl` forced on; Q8_0 spills): pp2048 -7.9% SQ_BUSY_CYCLES (1.3811e11 -> 1.2713e11; FFN only -5.7%), pp8192 -3.5% cycles (first wiring, before the tall epilogue). The afrag kstore / kres first used direct fragment stores (each lane 8 rows of one token at a 2-row stride: 128 scattered stores per wave; on the K = 6144 kres the stores cost 15% and the residual loads 14%); `tallepi` stages them through 16-row LDS slabs in the free weight tile (b128 stores, a fence per slab or the residual loads are hoisted to 256 VGPRs): kstore IQ4_XS -12.9%, IQ3_XXS -11.3%, IQ3_S -9.1%, Q3_K -7.2%, kres K = 17408 -8.2..-10.4%, K = 6144 IQ3_S -8.7% (was +0.4%), hidden bit-identical; 1000 tokens -1.5%; 300 tokens keep the narrow tiles, where 512-token tiles were +2.4%. The best `lhs_stream` depends on the decoder's register allocation: IQ3_XXS without it waited vmcnt(0) twice per phase on the step-1 B prefetch (kstore -0.2% -> -5.5% with 4, swiglu -8.0% -> -12.1% with 2). Q3_K / Q4_K were +11 / +25% only from rhs-outer pushing them past 192 VGPRs; without it Q3_K kstore is -2.8%, Q4_K +-0 (the B prefetch needs 208 VGPRs for their decoders): only the decoded weights live in LDS, each wave does 128 rows x 32 tokens (2 activation fragments per k step, loaded from the input), 16 waves x 32 = 512 tokens per decoded weight (decode VALU per WMMA 4.3 -> 2.2). Needs a fragment-major input (`Tile.atiled`: each 16 x 16 tile 512 contiguous bytes; row-major input is +36%, 16 rows 10 KB apart per load), KSUB 128 so all 16 waves decode, and the stagger on every grid (lockstep co-resident workgroups: kres +2.9% instead of -3.5%, kstore +7.2%). Then B-load latency: each k step waited on its own loads. Fixed by `bpre` 2 (next step's B fragments before this step's MMAs), `wlate` (next phase's weight loads in the last k step after its B loads, so they free 8 VGPRs in the MMA section and in-order vmcnt does not chain B waits to DRAM; without dropping the decode's fence there it hit 224 VGPRs) and `b0early` (step 0's B before the decode): 192 VGPRs, two workgroups per WGP. Bit-identical (gemm_bench `input_tiled`). Clock-free vs production: swiglu IQ4_XS -11.7%, IQ3_S -11.1% (plain swiglu epilogue: the LDS one needs 32-row waves), IQ3_XXS -7.6%; kstore IQ4_XS -6.5%, IQ3_S -3.7% (640 rows -1.9%), IQ3_XXS -0.8%; kres K = 17408 IQ4_XS -5.7%, IQ3_S -5.1%, IQ3_XXS -0.2%; kres K = 6144 +1.4%; Q4_K +25%; kqg refused by the footprint gate at BN 512. About -3.7% of GEMM cycles if wired in. IQ3_S kstore is at 87% of the WMMA floor (production 84%); what is left (ablations): barriers 4.8%, B-load issue 4.2% (32 b128 per wave per phase; WMMA B operands are replicated across half-waves), decode VALU ~2.2 cycles per WMMA. 1024-token tiles +9%, A streaming no gain.
- Mixed-format fused gate / up (`gen_gemm_tile` fmt "<gate>:<up>", kind ffn; off): the up rows decode with the up format's decoder, generated under its own configuration; one binding spans both tensors (ffn_up follows ffn_gate in the GGUF). Bit-identical (gemm_bench `<gate>+<up>` jobs, real IQ3_S + IQ4_XS layer). It loses to the two afrag GEMMs: carrying both formats' prefetched loads takes 208 VGPRs (one workgroup per WGP: +9%); loading each wave's format at its decode (`Tile.ffn_inload`) fits 192 only without the B prefetch and is +3.1%.
- GEMM barriers (two per phase) cost 2.7% (IQ4_XS kstore) to 4.4% (IQ3_S kres K = 17408) by ablation. No fix fits gfx11: one barrier needs a double-buffered weight tile (KSUB 64 leaves half the threads idle in decode: -7.9% vs -12.8%; KSUB 128 needs 70 KB of LDS), KSUB 256 needs 67.6 KB at 128 rows and doubles the B loads per WMMA at 64 rows, and gfx11 has no named barriers.
- DeltaNet (chunked) forward substitution: one wave computes T = (I + A)^-1 while the other seven wait at a barrier (43.5% of wave time at barriers, a third at that one). The compiler waited on each A-column LDS load right before its fmas (two in flight: the scheduler charges an LDS load ~1 cycle), so the LDS latency was paid 136 times per chunk on the critical wave. Loading the columns 2 steps ahead behind a fence (`gen_gdn_chunk.TPRE`): DeltaNet 1.571 -> 1.487 ms standalone, pp2048 -0.35% cycles, bit-identical (3 steps hits 256 VGPRs, 4 spills: 3.4 ms). Storing -A to drop the 496 negations on that wave was bit-identical but no faster (latency-bound, not issue-bound). The chunk-boundary waits were not input latency (the next chunk is already prefetched) but store drains: the compiler waits for every outstanding global store (s_waitcnt_vscnt 0) before the decay scan's ds_bpermute and before global loads (no alias tracking), so the chunk's O stores stalled wave 0, the critical wave, at the next chunk's start. `OLATE` stores each chunk's O in the next iteration, after the scan and the next-chunk loads are issued, unconditionally (inside an `if ci > 0` the branch join drained the loads just issued: +8%; at chunk 0 it stores zeros that chunk 1 overwrites, same thread and address): DeltaNet 1.541 -> 1.468 ms, 256 VGPRs (same granule as 248), bit-identical incl. the GPU reference; pp2048 -0.07% cycles. What is left is round quantization: 96 workgroups (48 heads x 2 row blocks), 63.7 KB of LDS each, so 2 per WGP: 40 slots and 3 rounds for 2.4 rounds of work (40 workgroups 0.532 ms, 80 1.057, 96 1.403). Two rounds (3 per WGP) would be ~25% of DeltaNet (~0.7% of prefill) and needs <= 42.6 KB: the K / Q rows, K^T, V^T, the f16 state copy (17.4 KB), A, P and T are all live in phases 2-5 (peak ~58-63 KB; K^T and the state copy exist because gfx11 has no transposing LDS loads), padding is ~7 KB, and 32-row workgroups duplicate the row-independent work (the solve).
- Conv -> DeltaNet hand-off in f16 (`gen_gdn_chunk.CONV16`, paired `convkq.hal`): DeltaNet rounded its inputs to f16 itself (f16(k * inv_k), f16(q * q_scale), f16(v)), so the conv now does those same f32 products and roundings (the q / k workgroup already holds the whole head and computes the scales) and stores f16; DeltaNet only moves the rows into LDS. Bit-identical, half the conv_out bytes. Two latency traps found with rocprof-studio on the way: the conv's per-tap `if t >= k` loads drained vmcnt(0) at each branch join (4 serial DRAM trips per wave; now all loads issue unconditionally and select), and an `extf` inside the prefetched loop-carry forced the prefetch's wait at the back edge (the raw f16 rows are carried and used as is). pp2048 conv 85 -> 71.5 M, DeltaNet 156 -> 146 M cycles, total -0.84% (counter run; the clean bench -0.4% cycles). An f16 qkv projection output + f16 conv input on top (YAH_F16X experiment, not kept) reached -1.1% but is not bit-exact: T1 gate kl_mean 1.26e-6 (limit 1.3e-7), p99.9 5.2e-5, 0 flips, ppl unchanged.
- IQ2_XXS / IQ2_XS afrag GEMMs (ffn_gate of layers 3, 9, 14, ffn_up of layer 5): the grid / sign lookups leave no room for B prefetch (bpre 1-2: 208-216 VGPRs, one workgroup per WGP); without it 192 VGPRs: kstore IQ2_XXS -19.3%, IQ2_XS -18.4%, swiglu IQ2_XXS -16.6% standalone, pp2048 iq2 families 105.1 -> 87.8 M cycles (-0.3% of prefill), bit-identical.
- RMS norm weights staged in LDS once per workgroup (`gen_half_norm` wlds; 16-byte loads), the output pass reads them from LDS instead of each row walking the 20 KB vector through L2 two loads at a time: norm 0.399 -> 0.359 ms standalone, pp2048 norm family 125.0 -> 117.1 M cycles, bit-identical. Counters (rocprof-compute): the norm is latency-bound, not bandwidth-bound (63 MB in 0.4 ms, 44% of wave cycles in s_waitcnt; reloads past `keep` miss L2 and re-read 27 MB from DRAM). Its bit-exact contract (lane l sums elements l, l + 32, ... in order) pins it to 4-byte loads and one wave per row; staging the row in LDS does not fit (20 KB per row).
- RMS norm rows split over two waves (`gen_half_norm.gen_split`, dispatch.txt `norm_split`): wave p holds elements [p n/2, (p+1) n/2) of its lanes, and lane l's sum of squares stays one chain in element order (wave 1 continues wave 0's partial from LDS after a barrier; branch-free: every wave runs its chain, only the live part's result is used), so the bits are unchanged. Half the values per wave: more waves and loads in flight. Standalone 0.383 -> 0.299 ms per 2048 rows (~214 GB/s, at the DRAM limit; 4 waves per row the same), pp2048 norm family 117.1 -> 98.7 M cycles, bit-identical on every reference set. The driver divides the norm grid by the split (a set without the row is read as split 1).
- Attention GQA packing restored as the default (`emit_prefill_pp` YAH_ATTN_SHAPE, `gen_attn_fa.configure`): a workgroup runs all 6 query heads of a KV head x 16 tokens (12 waves; the last 256 threads stage V, the first 256 K, LDS stores behind wave-uniform guards, loads unconditional), so each K / V tile is staged once for 6 heads (4x less K / V traffic). Bit-identical to 2 x 32 (pp2048, pp8192, the 32K fp16 / kv8 references). pp2048 attention 90.6 -> 86.2 M cycles (-4.9%), pp8192 1051.9 vs 1053.8 M (even). The 2026-10-01 loss (+2.8%) was a standalone pp8192 call on the earlier kernel; the cleanup (ea6e054) had removed the option along with its staging guards and head map.
- RoPE hands the attention f16(q / 16) (`emit_prefill_pp.rope_q16`, `gen_attn_fa.Q16`, dispatch.txt `q16`; not with kv4, whose H256 rotates the f32 Q): the attention's own Q scale and rounding moved to where Q is produced, into a separate f16 buffer (in place would race). Half the Q bytes written and read: pp2048 attention 86.7 -> 81.4 M cycles, RoPE 27.6 -> 26.5 M, bit-identical on every reference set.
- RoPE without its dead stores (`emit_prefill_pp.rope_live`): nothing in the prefill reads the f32 K / V cache copies or the in-place f32 k_out (the attention reads the f16 caches; decode has its own): ~24 MB of writes per attention layer gone, RoPE 26.5 -> 24.0 M cycles, bit-identical incl. the prefill -> decode references. (kc32_ / vc32_ are still allocated for the unused bindings.)
- Attention at 4 workgroups per WGP and conflict-free V staging (`gen_attn_fa` QDIRECT, VSWZ, QKF 1; shape back to 2 x 32): Q^T fragments load straight from the f16 query (lane = query row, one 32-B load per fragment), so the LDS Q stage, its 4-way-conflicted stores, two prologue barriers and the drain store are gone and LDS drops to 28.9 KB; one K fragment in flight (QKF 1) brings the kernel to 192 VGPRs: 4 x 8 waves per WGP instead of 3 (or 2 x 12 packed). V^T staging thread t stages dim (t%2)*8 + t/2 into LDS row t (same contents; each 8-lane store pass hits 8 disjoint bank groups instead of 2-way conflicts). Standalone (non-paged, random data) 1.963 -> 1.792 ms. In the paged fragment-major build (wmma_t, what pp2048 runs) VSWZ tipped the allocator to 236 VGPRs at a scheduled pressure of 184 (fragmentation); recomputing the swizzled V offset per tile instead of keeping it live through the loop (VSWZR) brings it to 192 with the swizzle (pp2048 wmma_t: 236 VGPRs with VSWZ 71.7 M, 192 without it 77.9 M, 192 with VSWZR 68.9 M). pp2048 attention 80.4 -> 68.9 M cycles, pp8192 1051.9 -> 941.1 M, bit-identical on every reference set. Tried on the way, bit-identical but slower: FA3 2-stage pipeline (QK of tile j+1 beside softmax / P.V of tile j, double K buffer; 2.01-2.09 vs 1.96 ms), more K fragments in flight, V fragments loaded ahead of the softmax, per-fragment gate loads.
- Attention diagonal-only mask and gate fence (2026-10-05, `gen_attn_fa` DIAG_MASK, GATE_FENCE; clock-free interleaved bench yah-scratch/attnp/attnab.py + attn_bench.cc, SQ_BUSY_CYCLES, noise ~0.3%): the causal / context mask moved under a wave-uniform branch taken only on tiles where some lane has a key past its limit or a dead row (-1.3%); the epilogue's 16 gate loads now issue behind one fence (the allocator had recycled each address register right after its load and Loom waited for the load before the overwrite: ~6 serialized DRAM round trips). Together standalone -1.7%, pp2048 attention 30.51 -> 30.00 ms, bit-identical (pp2048 hidden / logits, 32K fp16 and kv8 prefill -> decode). PMC: the kernel is issue-bound, WMMA ~58% and VALU ~28% of SIMD time (SQ_INST_CYCLES_VALU), 0 LDS bank conflicts; per-wave ATT stalls (V-load waits, the epilogue's gate waits) are hidden by the other 7 waves: no V load at all +0.5%, no causal mask -0.9..-2.7%. Bit-identical but no gain: gate loads off one address register via per-fragment views, 4-wide halves with a fence after them, the gate sigmoid via v_rcp + Newton (recip1), K staging without the redundant zeroing (-0.1% vs -1.3% with it: fewer VALU, slower), V(ks + 16) carried across the back edge (200 VGPRs, +129 moves: the allocator's carried-prefetch copies), 2 x 64 and 6 x 16 shapes (+17.5% each: half the K / V traffic does not help). o * (1 / l) in the epilogue (gate N): +2% with barrier waits +17% (unexplained).
- kv4 / kv8 attention decode as v_and + v_lshl_add (2026-10-05, `gen_attn_fa` DEC_LSHADD): a left-shifted nibble pair ((w << s) & M) | C equals ((w & (M >> s)) << s) + C (the fields do not overlap), which Loom selects as a fused shift-add: kv4 loop 296 -> 288 VALU per tile, attention at 32K depth 140.7 -> 139.6 M cycles (-0.76%), bit-identical (kv4 126K-depth slot logits, kv8 32K prefill -> decode); fp16 untouched. Long-context harness: yah-scratch/attnl (paged kv4 / fp16 at any depth, clock-free). At 32K depth kv4 runs at 59% of the WMMA floor and fp16 at 69%: kv4's int4 decode costs ~89 VALU per tile (shift, and, or, packed fma); Loom has no v_and_or_b32 descriptor, so the right-shifted pairs stay at 3 VALU. Packing the GQA group (6 x 16) ties for kv4 (2 workgroups per WGP), loses 7.9% for fp16.
- Attention probes after the 4-workgroup kernel (standalone 1.783 ms, all bit-identical, all slower): K(j+2) / V(j+1) loaded at the end of tile j and carried across the back edge 1.860 ms (Loom drains vmcnt(0) at the back edge); V loaded before K and K staged at the end of phase B 1.905 ms (the rescale branch's join drains the K load); K and V^T fragments straight from global (each lane's operand row is 32 contiguous bytes; same row order, so bit-identical; LDS 16 KB, one barrier per tile) 2.152 ms with one K fragment in flight, 2.014 ms with all 8: unlike the afrag GEMM's per-wave B fragments, every K / V fragment here is shared by the workgroup's 4 query blocks, so direct loads multiply the L0 requests ~8x (128 vs 16 KB per tile) and expose a load latency per WMMA. LDS staging is the right tool for attention's operands.
- Two-micro-batch pipeline (two 1024-token chunks in one graph, the second a step behind, so one's memory-bound kernels run beside the other's GEMMs; not shipped, patch in yah-scratch/overlap/pair-pipeline.patch): exact (2 x 1024 chunked = 1 x 2048 bit for bit, last row and logits), but no gain: pp2048 2769 ms vs 2813 serial 2 x 1024 and ~2780 for one chunk. A probe showed a norm 65% hidden beside a GEMM, and with a segment-aligning merge (HRX drains at every dependency, so two kernels overlap only inside a barrier segment) DeltaNet ran 32% beside GEMMs, norms 61%, conv 52%, yet the span did not move: the memory-bound kernels stretched instead (DeltaNet 89 -> 102 ms). The afrag GEMMs fill the register file (two 16-wave workgroups at 192 VGPRs per WGP), so nothing shares a SIMD with them: overlap is a spatial split of WGPs, zero-sum against a compute-bound GEMM; only GEMM tails are free, and they are small (~18 ms idle per pp2048).

- Wave priority by phase (2026-10-05, s_setprio binary-patched over the first s_delay_alu after each K-loop barrier, same code size, hashes identical; yah-scratch/prio/prio.py): MMA phase 3 / decode 0: IQ3_XXS kstore 640 +0.36%, Q4_K +0.19%; decode 3 / MMA 0: -0.20%, -0.77%: within noise, no consistent direction. The hardware's arbitration already interleaves the two workgroups; no Loom op for it.
- Cost of the afrag output epilogue (2026-10-05, ablation: the tall epilogue replaced by a per-lane sum of the accumulators and one store, wrong output): kqg -2.9%, kstore 384 -1.8%, kstore 640 -0.4% (IQ4_XS), IQ3_XXS kstore 640 / IQ3_S kstore 1088 +4 / +5% (allocation reshuffled). Standalone efficiency is ~87-92% for every format regardless of loop VALU (IQ3_XXS kstore 640 92.2% at 114 VALU, IQ3_S 91.2% at 225); a persistent kstore / ffn hiding the epilogue would be worth < 0.5% of pp2048: not built.
- IQ4_XS decode through an LDS byte-pair table (2026-10-05): a 1 KB table maps each qs byte to its two codebook values as signed f16 subnormals, one ds_load_b32 per byte at a pre-scaled offset, fma_mix with a +0 addend: loop VALU 175 -> 122, LDS ops 132 -> 148, pp2048 output bit-identical. One-process A/B: kstore +1.3%, kqg +2.5%, swiglu +3.2%, kres -0.6%: the 16 random-address LDS loads per group cost more than the 53 VALU. (gemm_bench's synthetic-input hash differed on kstore 384 / 64 although the model output was bit-identical.) Not shipped.
- f16 residual stream (2026-10-04, quality simulation: every persistent-kres residual sum rounded to f16 and back before its f32 store): gate N fails, mean KL 6.8e-6 (limit 5.3e-7), p99.9 1.5e-4 (limit 1.8e-5), 0 flips, ln PPL -7e-5. The residual stream stays f32 (its 42 MB DRAM read is ~2.8% of pp2048 in the kres epilogues).
- f16 projection outputs (2026-10-04, quality simulation: kstore / kqg outputs rounded to f16 and back; the qkv's f16 hand-off had failed on 2026-10-03 at mean KL 1.26e-6): z, attention q / gate and k / v together fail gate N (mean KL 1.3e-6, p99.9 6.8e-5); the DeltaNet z gate alone fails too (1.05e-6, 4.7e-5). Any extra f16 rounding of a whole activation tensor costs ~1e-6 mean KL, 2x the N limit: N admits f32 reorders and fusions, not new f16 roundings.

Prefill attention, DeltaNet and pipeline:
- Scheduling the afrag GEMMs' LDS A-fragment loads further ahead (diagnosis compiler = pin + patches + env knobs; 2026-10-03): half the A loads are waited on with no WMMA in between (mean 0.7 WMMAs load-to-use). Charging the load's completion latency on its data edges (16 / 24 / 32 cycles) keeps outputs identical and helps where the kernel stays at 192 VGPRs (IQ3_S kstore 1088 20.51 -> 20.07 M, IQ3_S swiglu 21.23 -> 20.82 M, -2%), but 10 of 12 shapes allocate 200-208 VGPRs, drop to one workgroup per WGP and lose 9-27%. Workgroup-quantized residency cliffs in the scheduler (192 -> 193 VGPRs costs 8 -> 4 waves, not 8 -> 7) change nothing: the scheduler's estimate stays under 192 and the allocator adds the registers (loop-carried prefetch copies). bpre=1 does not make room. Upside without allocator work: ~0.2% of prefill.
- GEMM peak table (2026-10-03, pp2048 SQ_BUSY_CYCLES per family vs 34 cycles per WMMA over 80 SIMDs): all GEMMs 91.7% (475 M of 5716 M lost); kres the worst big family (IQ3_S 88%). Several small families (alpha / beta, attn_k / v) look poor but run overlapped inside the z / q projections' tails (dispatch timestamps), so their busy cycles are not wall time.
- VALU and WMMA share the SIMD (microbenchmark, 8 waves per SIMD, independent v_xor beside independent WMMA chains): 12.2 / 13.8 / 14.2 / 16.8 / 20.1 ns per WMMA per SIMD at 0 / 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 VALU per WMMA, ~2 cycles per VALU. The afrag GEMMs issue ~1.9 non-WMMA VALU per WMMA (decode, fragment v_movs): that is the bulk of their gap to the floor. Removing all IQ4_XS decode math (timing ablation, loads kept) is -4.1%.
- kres residual epilogue: the K = 6144 residual GEMM runs at 76.8% of floor, the same tile storing plain f32 (no residual read) at 90.7% (K = 17408: 88.4% vs 94.2%), ~2.7% of prefill over all 128 calls. Not latency (pipelining the residual loads `respre` 1-3: bit-identical, ISA shows vmcnt(4-5) instead of vmcnt(0), no gain) and not lockstep rounds (BN 256 / stagger: worse): the 42 MB residual read lands on the matrix-idle end of every 80-workgroup round. Moving the add into the following norm (`gen_half_norm` resadd: bitwise the same sum) costs the latency-bound norm +0.9 ms per call against -0.55 ms in the GEMM. The residual read itself is the cost: reading it from a cached 2 MB window (timing ablation) runs the kres at kstore speed (7.32 vs 8.62 M; 20.20 vs 21.38 M). Prefetching its lines in the last 4-16 k steps does not help: scalar loads (wave-uniform addresses via readfirstlane, no VGPRs) share lgkmcnt with LDS and stall the A-fragment waits (+18-50%); vector loads at the top of a step make every younger B load wait behind them (vmcnt is in order) and cost 8-16 VGPRs; one load per lane at the end of each step, consumed a step later, is nearly free (bpre 1: 9.03 vs 8.95 M) but the epilogue is no faster: lines beyond L2 do not come back at L2 speed.
- Half-tile double-buffered afrag GEMMs (KSUB 64 x 2 buffers, ~37 KB LDS: two workgroups per WGP still fit; each wave decodes the next half tile beside its MMAs on the current one): IQ4_XS kstore 20.95 vs 19.76 M, IQ3_S 21.49 vs 20.54 M (best: bpre 1, lhs_stream 2; wlate / b0early do not combine with dbuf, bpre 2 hits 200 VGPRs). The production tile already hides most decode behind the other workgroup on the WGP: ATT puts at most ~9% of SIMD time (inflated by trace overhead) with no wave inside an MMA run.
- Wave priority around the GEMM phases (HIP microbenchmark with the afrag GEMM's structure: 16-wave workgroups, 2 per WGP, barrier / VALU decode into a 34 KB LDS tile / barrier / 128 WMMAs per wave): s_setprio 3 in the decode phase +2.0%, in the MMA phase +2.8% (6.152 / 6.276 / 6.326 ms; control repeat 6.162). The arbiter already interleaves the phases; not worth a Loom op.
- Clock levers (sustained ~8 s runs at the 95 C cap; sustained clock varies ~5% between identical runs, so these are single readings): DRAM traffic does not set the clock (fused FFN with activations L2-resident: socket 106 -> 99 W, clock 2304 -> 2247 MHz). LDS reads cost power (WMMA microbenchmark, conflict-free, pipelined: 0 / 1 / 2 ds_load_b128 per WMMA: 18.9 / 26.8 / 36.3 W gfx, 34.8 / 36.4 / 55.5 cycles per WMMA, clock 2426 / 2325 / 2450 MHz). 64 x 64 wave tiles halve the GEMM's LDS reads and its GPU power (IQ4_XS kstore 30.3 -> 26.0 W) but not its clock (2266 vs 2269 MHz; the 95 C hotspot stays), and cost cycles even at 192 VGPRs (KSUB 128 with the plain epilogue and lhs_stream 1: +5.5%; KSUB 64: +9.4%), bit-identical. The tall epilogue costs the 64 x 64 tile 8 VGPRs (200 -> 192 without it).
- Attention at pp2048 (6 x 16 packed, 86.2 M cycles; timing ablations, outputs wrong on purpose): not VALU-bound: every tile unmasked (hot loop 223 -> 172 VALU per 16-key tile, LDS ops unchanged at 46) 86.4 M, so diagonal-only masking, address hoisting and softmax de-duplication cannot pay. Occupancy matters moderately: 2 x 32 at 3 vs 2 workgroups per WGP (LDS padded) 90.3 vs 96.0 M. Per-workgroup fixed cost (zero KV tiles) 29.7 M, of which the gate (f32 loads at the end + sigmoid) 13 M; exposed in the real kernel: no gate 80.7 M (-6.4%), no Q loads 80.1 M (confounded: Q = 0 also removes rescales), the o / l divisions nothing. Reviews of FlashAttention 1-4 and three block-sparse prefill papers (2026-10-03): no async copies / warp specialization / ping-pong on RDNA3.5, software exp a loss (exp ~3% of the loop), FP8 absent, sparse prefill approximate and only worth it from ~32K.
- Attention ATT (non-paged 6 x 16 Q16 build on random data, 1.96 ms vs ~2 ms in the pipeline; rocprof-studio): per-wave latency 63% in the tile loop (LDS waits 34%, VALU dependency stalls 31%, the two barriers 21%, global waits 7%), 37% in the prologue / epilogue (Q, gate and store waits). Load placement does not help: QKF 4 / 8 (more K fragments in flight) 1.950 / 1.998 vs 1.957 ms; V fragments loaded ahead of the softmax behind a fence 2.072 / 2.141 ms (4 / 8), and after the S partial loads 1.986 / 2.038 ms (2 / 4), all bit-identical. Remaining routes are structural (FA3 2-stage pipeline with two K buffers; a 4th workgroup per WGP via the 2 x 32 shape without the LDS Q stage and <= 192 VGPRs).
- GEMM tail rounds (2026-10-03): the DeltaNet z projection is 192 workgroups = 2.4 rounds of 80 slots, kqg 4.8, the 1088-row GEMMs 6.8, the fused FFN 13.6. Dispatch timestamps show the tails already absorbed: z starts 0.56 ms before the qkv GEMM ends, alpha / beta start inside z's tail, and the whole pp2048 has 11.8 ms idle (0.45%). Nothing to win from reshaping grids.
- 1024-token, 32-wave afrag workgroups (one decoded weight tile per WGP, half the decode per WMMA): KSUB 128 leaves half the lanes without a decode group (the generator has no guard; the footprint gate refused it), KSUB 256 needs 67,584 B of LDS > the 64 KiB per-workgroup limit (unpadded rows would need a swizzle). Not pursued: decode is not what limits these GEMMs (f16 table decode -0.7%, decode-free f16 weights +8%); rocprof-studio's hidden-latency model on the fused FFN puts the exposed time on decode VALU queued behind the matrix pipe (71%) and s_waitcnt (18%), barriers ~0.
- DeltaNet LDS diet for 3 workgroups per WGP: not worth it. 40 / 80 / 96 workgroups take 0.532 / 1.057 / 1.403 ms, linear per WGP (two workgroups on a WGP do not overlap), so round quantization costs ~9% of DeltaNet (~0.2% of prefill), not the ~25% estimated before.

- DeltaNet gate projection recorded beside the DeltaNet in the prefill graph: neutral (3036.8 vs 3032.0 ms). A graph barrier drains all earlier work, and the GEMM's workgroups only start once the DeltaNet's 96 are all placed (1.2 ms in).
- HIP-order attention, key-loop unroll(2): 80.59 -> 79.03 M cycles with 31 spill stores; not adopted.
- FA attention, 32-key tiles: 64.6 -> 72.5 M cycles; LDS 53.8 KB allows only 2 workgroups per WGP.
- FA attention, GQA packing (6 heads x 16 tokens, 12 waves): 64.6 -> 66.8 M cycles; 4x less DRAM, but less phase diversity with 2 x 12 waves.
- FA attention, threshold rescale (FA4, THR 8): 69.2 vs 67.1 M cycles exact; no gain and changes numerics.
- FA attention, Q staged in halves: 64.6 -> 66.1 M cycles; 200 VGPRs still miss the 4th workgroup.
- Integer-compute attention (kv4a4 iu4, kv4a8, int8 iu8): kv4a4 -14.5% attention (~0.4% of pp8192) for 96% 99%-precision; dropped for the a16 formats.
- uint8 P in attention: 16x the attention error (small weights round to 0); P stays f16.
- Page table in LDS: attention +1.1% (the cost is index math, not the scalar load), and it hung the GPU when read before its barrier.
- DeltaNet dual-issue FMAs (`vector.dotf`): +1.6-2.3% cycles; VOPD pairs across chains stretch the per-token latency path.
- FLA-style 3-kernel DeltaNet split: per-chunk state snapshots ~150 ms over 48 layers at pp8192 vs ~350 ms for all of DeltaNet.
- Chunked DeltaNet packed f16 pair stores: 3.702 -> 3.744 M cycles; the stores queue behind LDS traffic.
- Chunked DeltaNet, 2026-10-04 (80 workgroups standalone, f16 normalized inputs, outputs bit-compared): ATT puts ~20% of a chunk in wave 0's forward substitution (496 `v_fma_f32`, 136 `ds_load_b128`) with 7 waves at the barrier, and both co-resident workgroups' solving waves on the same SIMD. Moving the solve to wave 1 / 2 / 4 of odd workgroups (by x or y): the hardware places waves so that the two solves still share a SIMD (ATT of x1); timing differences were single-round noise (+-4%). Inputs prefetched two chunks ahead: 248 VGPRs, +8% (the rotation copies the fresh loads at the back edge, so the `vmcnt(0)` stays); a cache-hot-prefetch ablation is only -4%, so DRAM latency is minor (ATT inflates it). `scalar.fmaf` as tied `v_fmac_f32` when the addend dies (Loom contract rule, worktree branch loom-fmac): 496 VOP3 fmas -> 237 `v_dual_fmac_f32` + 22, -171 instructions, bit-identical, 0.917 vs 0.920 ms and pp2048 DeltaNet 158.6 vs 158.9 M cycles: the solve is LDS / dependency latency, not issue. Attention's 512 fmas keep VOP3 (shared addends).
- Chunked DeltaNet solve, value of a faster one (2026-10-05, ablation T = I, wrong output, same loads / stores / barriers): 80-workgroup kernel 0.921 -> 0.843 ms (-8.5%; ATT's ~20% was trace-inflated), so even a free solve is ~0.23% of pp2048 and a blocked 16 x 16 inverse (two more barriers) maybe half of that: not built.
- Weights as a device-local copy or a 2 MB THP copy: 3505 / 3522 vs 3527 ms (noise), load +5 s / +43 s; GEMMs reuse each weight tile 2048 times.
- Load-time tricks on the GGUF mapping: prefault on a thread (device init 180 -> 437 ms, it holds `mmap_lock`), `MADV_HUGEPAGE` (cold load +0.6 s), `MADV_POPULATE_READ` (-30 ms only).
- Freeing host power (no busy-poll) for GPU clock: no clock gain; the GPU is thermal-limited, not power-limited.
- HIP graph capture / persistent kernels for prefill: ~0.02%; a kernel boundary is free when the kernel has real work.

Decode:

- Band-fused input projections: 61.17 vs 61.20 ms, neutral (176 fewer dispatches; the separate no-barrier dispatches were already ~92% efficient). On by default.
- RMSNorm folded into the residual GEMV's last workgroup: 61.30 vs 61.18 ms; the ~5 us serial norm tail cancels the ~3.4 us launch it removes.
- Read-ahead depth 3 / 4 on the GEMV loop: 66.1 / 67.8 vs 63.1 ms (depth 2); the queue costs registers.
- 32-element groups per lane: 61.77 vs 60.48 ms; 11-31% fewer VALU but 60-75 -> 103-114 VGPRs.
- 16 lanes per row: 63.49 vs 60.19 ms; shorter rows lose bandwidth.
- Persistent GEMVs (workgroups loop over row groups): 61.67 vs 60.48 ms; ragged last round (SwiGLU +3.7%).
- Persistent megakernel: grid barrier 0.26-0.49 us would save at most ~1.3 ms per token, but persistent GEMVs lose and the register count is the max over all phases; not built.
- HRX graph replay: 3.32 vs 3.57 us per dependent dispatch; only fewer dependent dispatches help.
- Band and plain GEMV geometry (rows / waves per workgroup 1 x 4, 2 x 8, 4 x 4, 4 x 2; `YAH_TILES` "rw"): 60.6-62.6 vs 60.27 ms for the default 2 x 4; every step's logits identical.
- SwiGLU geometry (rows / waves per workgroup 1 x 4 .. 2 x 8): all within 0.2 ms; residual GEMV 2 x 4 beats 1 x 4, 2 x 8, 1 x 8, 4 x 4 by 1-1.5 ms.
- LDS sign-mask table instead of `v_mul_lo_u32`: neutral; after word decode the GEMVs are not VALU-bound.
- `index.assume` instead of load clamps: -4% VALU, neutral.
- Explicit fma chain for the 16-element dot: same VALU count; the compiler already contracts.
- No x loads at all (ablation): -0.45 ms; x traffic does not limit the low-bit GEMVs.
- kv4a16 decode attention vs kv8a16: no faster (347 vs 362 us at 30.7K) despite 40% fewer bytes; per-workgroup q rotation, barriers and group sums make it latency-bound.

KV codecs (fake-quantized through a KV hook since removed, 32K):

- UltraQuant MXFP4 K and V: mean KL 1.0e-2, dPPL +0.47%; per-token MXFP4 V is 4x worse than per-channel tile V.
- WUSH calibrated transform for int4 K: only ~7% better than a fixed H256 Hadamard; the rotation does the work.
- KVarN Sinkhorn balancing: as good as a rotation alone, redundant with one.
- Quantile clipping for int4 K: 4x worse than range shrink.
